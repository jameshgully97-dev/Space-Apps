    About the data

    Conflict events come from the GDELT 2.0 Event Database, which machine-codes world news every 15 minutes. Events are plotted where the article places the action; country-level geocodes are dropped. Coding is automated and can be wrong, duplicated or refer to past events — open the source articles before relying on any single marker.

    Verification. GDELT codes news automatically, so figurative language ("legal battle", "fight over the budget", "playoff clash") can be coded as combat. Every event is checked against the headlines of all the articles that mention it. Verified events have coverage that uses unambiguous physical-violence terms (killed, shelling, airstrike, gunmen, explosion…); words like battle, fight, attack, war, strike or clash never count on their own. Filtered events read as legal, political, sports, business, health or entertainment news and are hidden by default. Everything else is Unverified: shown faded, never animated. When the coverage names what happened (e.g. "shelling"), that is shown instead of the machine coding. Headlines are read in English only, so non-English coverage stays Unverified.

    Who acted. Every event is classified by the actor GDELT codes as its initiator, never by the kind of violence: Military needs an explicit armed-forces code (MIL); Armed group means rebels, insurgents, separatists or unaligned armed forces; Police means law enforcement (COP; police wins if an actor carries both codes); everything else, including events attributed only to a country, a group of civilians or nobody, is Unattributed. Because the rule is strict, some genuine military actions will appear as Unattributed when the news coding names only a country.

    Soldiers, tanks, artillery, jets, drones and ships shown around an event are symbolic animations of the reported event type, and they are only drawn for military and armed-group events. Police events show officers and patrol cars; unattributed events show no people or vehicles. No public source publishes live positions of individual troops or vehicles, and this app does not attempt to. Soldiers wear a stylised version of their military's camouflage with a flag patch (and field identification armbands, e.g. Ukrainian yellow and Russian white tape); tanks carry national colours. The opposing side is drawn in its own kit when GDELT identifies it as a military or police force. Skin tones are varied at random within every group and are never assigned by nationality.

    Aircraft are real, live ADS-B positions from community receivers (adsb.lol, airplanes.live), drawn with a silhouette matching the reported ICAO aircraft type. Many military aircraft fly without transponders over active war zones, so coverage there is partial.

    GhostNet is S2 Underground's plan for off-grid HF radio nets (GhostNet v1.5, CC BY-NC-SA 4.0). The 📻 panel shows its schedule, frequencies and which comms window is on air. Stations shown on the globe are JS8Call reception reports on GhostNet frequencies from PSKReporter, refreshed every 5 minutes and located to their Maidenhead grid square. Being heard on a GhostNet frequency does not mean a station is taking part in GhostNet; 80 m reports can include ordinary JS8 traffic near 3.578 MHz.

    Imagery: Esri World Imagery, NASA Black Marble night lights, Natural Earth II, and optionally Google Photorealistic 3D Tiles.
